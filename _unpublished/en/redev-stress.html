Topic 2.4 · Economics

Stress-testing the financial model

Red team, sensitivity, the legal perimeter, and the economics on one sheet. The calculations stay in the spreadsheet.

Part 1 · Red teamPart 2 · Legal perimeterBoth tracks
Why

A stress test against wishful thinking

Key points

  • the model may add up but rest on a single assumption;
  • the stress test shows where the decision breaks down;
  • without this step, the risk of error becomes visible only after the investment;
  • the output is a list of checks and thresholds, not a new calculation.
Takeaway: A stress test reduces the risk of a decision built on a single assumption.
TermsStress test: checks a decision as assumptions worsen.
In short: what happens if the assumption fails.Assumption: a premise accepted in the model without supporting data.
In short: a number taken without a document.
Context

Where the stress test comes from

Key points

  • a red team looks for weak points instead of confirming the decision;
  • sensitivity analysis changes one assumption at a time and looks at the result;
  • this is how the market tests investment models before a decision;
  • the findings go back into the spreadsheet, not into a chat.
Takeaway: The check runs from a weak assumption to its effect.
TermsRed team: the role that looks for weak points in a decision.
In short: the people who look for where the decision breaks.Sensitivity: how the result changes when one assumption changes.
In short: how much the result depends on one number.

Task A · Prompt

The red team prompt

Act as the red team of an investment committee. I have uploaded a financial model spreadsheet: revenue, CAPEX, OPEX, and assumptions with their sources. Find the 5–7 weakest assumptions. For each one, state the assumption, why it is weak, what needs to be checked and against which source, and how the result will change (direction, not figures). Do not recalculate the model or invent new numbers; do not state values that are not in the spreadsheet.
Takeaway: The prompt limits the model to finding checks, not calculating.

Walk-away conditions

Walk-away conditions and sensitivity

Key points

  • walk-away conditions are formulated before the calculation;
  • the participant sets the threshold; the deck does not;
  • change one assumption at a time;
  • if the threshold is crossed, the decision changes.
Takeaway: A stress test answers at what change the decision stops working.
TermsSensitivity: how the result changes when one assumption changes.
In short: how much the result depends on one number.Assumption: a premise accepted in the model without supporting data.
In short: a number taken without a document.
Lawyer

Questions for the lawyer: what and why

Key points

  • PZZ: which use is permitted and on what basis;
  • up-to-date technical passport: which areas and structural elements are confirmed;
  • ZOUIT: which restrictions apply to the site and the building;
  • the lawyer's answer goes back into the model as an assumption or a prohibition.

Example: C52: 2005 BTI floor plans and 2016 measured drawings are available; the up-to-date technical passport, PZZ, and ZOUIT have no data.

Takeaway: A question to the lawyer closes a gap; it does not replace the document.
TermsPZZ: the city's land use and development rules.
In short: what can be built on the site.BTI: the bureau of technical inventory, which holds the technical passport and floor plans.
In short: the official technical drawings of the building.ZOUIT: zones with special conditions of territory use.
In short: restrictions on the site.
