Why

Why a crash test

What matters

  • the crash test checks whether the solution holds up under comparison;
  • materials and figures may contradict each other;
  • the cost of an error is a solution that fails expert review;
  • the result is a list of inconsistencies, not a new project.
Takeaway: The crash test catches discrepancies before the document is submitted for review.
TermsCrash test — a check of whether a solution holds up when compared with codes and calculations.
In plain terms: a stress check before review.
Context

Cross-check

What matters

  • cross-checking comes from review practice;
  • renders, tables, codes, and CAPEX are compared;
  • this is how documents are prepared for review in practice;
  • the check does not replace the codes or the calculation.
Takeaway: The comparison runs along several axes at once.
TermsReview — a second look at the work against set criteria.
In plain terms: a partner checking your work.

Task A · Prompt

Prompt for the visual audit

I have uploaded a render and the CAPEX table. List the structures and elements visible in the render (mezzanines, glazing, landscaping and public realm, engineering elements). For each one, state whether there is a line item in the CAPEX table and whether the floor area balance contradicts it. Describe only what is visible; if you are unsure, write “needs human review”.
Takeaway: The prompt limits the answer to what is visible and forbids guesses.

Review

Cross-review

Five comments following the scheme

  • what I see in the render or the table;
  • why it matters for consistency;
  • what I propose to check;
  • comments are written on a partner's work from the other track;
  • every comment is checked against the table.
Takeaway: The review checks a partner's work against the table, not against impressions.
