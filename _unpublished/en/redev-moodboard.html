Mood board and visual language

References, a style vocabulary, an image series, and assembling the mood board. Work happens in the browser.

Why

Why a project needs a mood board

What matters

  • a mood board records the agreement on style between the project and the visuals;
  • a style mistake costs a redo of the series and lost time;
  • without a mood board, each frame interprets the function differently;
  • the outcome is a vocabulary of features and boundaries, not “beautiful”.
Bottom line: A mood board reduces the risk of rework before the series begins.
TermsMood board — a board of visual references that sets the mood and visual language of the project.
In short: a board of samples for the project style.Visual language — a set of recurring features (materials, color, light, rhythm) by which the style is recognized.
In short: recurring style features.
Context

Where the mood board comes from

What matters

  • the mood board came from design practice as a working document, not a picture;
  • a reference sets features; a copy repeats a specific object;
  • a stock photo license determines what can be published and how to credit the author;
  • in practice, the mood board is approved before the series is generated.
Bottom line: A reference and a copy differ in rights and in how closely they repeat.

Terms

Stock photos, licenses, references

Three pillars of selection

  • a stock photo site is a catalog of photos with terms of use;
  • a license sets the terms on which the rights holder permits use;
  • a reference is a sample that informs the creation of an image;
  • only an image with a permissive license is suitable for publication.
Bottom line: The source and license are recorded before selection.
TermsStock photo site — a catalog of photographs with a license for use.Reference — a sample that informs the creation of an image.

Task A · Selection rules

Selection rules

Limits of the selection

  • 6–9 references, one of them the site itself;
  • for publication, only a license that permits use;
  • Pinterest remains a source of ideas, not of publication;
  • every reference has a source and a license;
  • what we take into the visual language: material, color, light, rhythm, scale.
Bottom line: Selection is limited by the task and by image rights.

Task B · Part 1

Build a style vocabulary

Input and outcome
