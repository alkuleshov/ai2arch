Three lenses

Surroundings, audience, activity

Surroundings

Context of place. What buildings, functions, and activities already exist on and around the site.

Audience

The human factor. Who lives, works, studies, and spends time in this location.

Activity

Spatial dynamics. How people use the area and what routes and visit patterns form. Traffic: through and destination.

TermsThrough traffic — people pass by; destination traffic — people come specifically to the site.
Why it matters

What analysis gives you

1

Identify existing and missing functions.

2

Reveal potential user groups.

3

Establish the needs of different groups.

4

Determine how the site can be used.

5

Draft preliminary functional scenarios.

Important: so you do not design functions just because they look good in a render.
Risks

What breaks without analysis

Typical mistakes

  • choosing functions without regard for the surroundings;
  • targeting an abstract audience;
  • duplicating existing services;
  • ignoring real routes;
  • spaces nobody uses.

Chain of risk

A decision without analysis →
a possible mistake →
consequences for the site

Example: a premium spa in a location without an audience or parking → CAPEX for equipment and waterproofing → OPEX on empty floor area (teaching example, not C52 data).

TermsCAPEX — capital expenditures: one-time investment in building or renovating the site.OPEX — operating expenses: regular payments to maintain the site.
C52 case

The site: main building 52a

The main building is at Suvorova St., 52a. The cluster area covers 52 and 52a.

Excluded: the site itself and its tenants.

Research question: which functions are logically justified here?

Analysis radius: you choose it; the example is a 15-minute walk.

What we assess: functions of neighboring plots, built form, attractors, accessibility.

C52 example; use the same algorithm for your site.

Algorithm

Overpass Turbo: seven steps

Steps

  1. Find the area.
  2. Define the object category.
  3. Go to Overpass Turbo.
  4. Build a query by OSM tags.
  5. Limit the query to the area.
  6. Run it and review the result.
  7. Export to GeoJSON.

What you get

  • building — footprints;
  • highway=footway and highway=pedestrian — pedestrian links;
  • amenity, shop, leisure — functions;
  • GeoJSON export.
TermsOverpass Turbo — a web tool for querying OSM objects by tag.OSM tag — a key=value pair that describes an object.GeoJSON — a file format for spatial data.

Strava: digital traces

What you can get

  • desire paths;

  • directions of movement;

How to get it

  1. Open Strava Global Heatmap.
  2. Find your site (C52 example).
  3. Choose the pedestrian or cycling layer.
  4. Choose the city scale and select routes near the site.

What to look at: where a route is through traffic and where it is sports activity.

TermsStrava Global Heatmap — a heat map of users’ GPS tracks.

Filter

Legally permissible?

What we check

  • Land use and development rules (PZZ) and permitted use;
  • building codes (SP) and restrictions;
  • protected zones;
  • fire and other requirements.

What we do

  • rule out what is not permitted;
  • clarify what is contested;
  • record assumptions.
A scenario without legal review is a hypothesis, not a scenario.
TermsPZZ — land use and development rules.Permitted use type — the permitted use of a land plot.SP — a code of practice, technical construction standards.

What we check

  • facts against primary sources;
