Roles

Redevelopment is teamwork

Architect

  • reads context and constraints;
  • proposes functions and scenarios;
  • assembles the architectural and visual study.

Developer

  • tests demand and economics;

What to do: record them in the data sheet; if there is no data, put them in row 7, “What we do not know”.

What to do: record it in the data sheet; if there is no data, put it in row 7, “What we do not know”.

Two tracks

One base, different deliverables

Track A · designer

Architectural and visual study

What you do

  • context of place and meanings;
  • functional scenarios;
  • floor area balance;
  • visual language and adaptation of the property.

What you deliver

  • a study of your property;
  • scenarios and rationale;
  • visual packaging;
  • the Track A final document.
Track B · developer

Investment concept data sheet

What you do

  • market and competitors;
  • product hypothesis;
  • floor area balance and economics;
  • risks and scenarios.

What you deliver

  • a concept data sheet for your property;
  • a financial model;
  • a teaser for a partner or investor;
  • the Track B final document.
Shared base

One logic, different deliverables

Context

PZZ (land use and development rules), surroundings, audience.

HBU

5–7 scenarios and a choice.

Economics

Floor areas, calculations, validation.

Document

The result of your track.

Choosing a track does not change the property. It changes your role and the final document.

Choice

How to choose a track

1

What document you need at the end: an architectural and visual study or an investment concept data sheet?

2

Where your strength lies: volumes and ergonomics, or numbers and rates?

3

Whom you will present to: an architectural council or an investor?

4

What you want to develop: design form or economics?

If in doubt: choose the track by your role, not by the picture.
Pitfalls

What gets in the way of choosing

“I'll take both tracks”
No. One track and one final document. Otherwise there will not be enough time for depth.
“I'll choose the property first, then the track”
The property and the track are fixed together: your role determines which data you need.
“I'll change the property later”
The property is chosen before Topic 1.1 and does not change until Demo Day.

Property and data sheet

C52 or your own property

Property data sheet

Why you need a data sheet

01

Fixing

One property and its source data.

02

Validation

What is confirmed by documents and what is a hypothesis.

03

Start

Topic 1.1 and all further work begin with the data sheet.

The data sheet is not a presentation. It is a one-page working card for the property.
Composition

What the data sheet includes

01

Identification

Address, cadastral number, type, name, designated use, area, block, date, value.

02

Physical parameters

Year, number of floors, wall material, structure, column spacing, heights, floor areas.

03

Constraints

Territorial zone, permitted use type (VRI), maximum parameters, ZOUIT, cultural heritage protection zones.

04

Rights

Right holder, type of right, encumbrances.

05

Engineering

Electrical capacity, heat, water and sewerage, insolation.

06

Current condition

In use or vacant, tenants, signs of disrepair.

07

What we do not know

A list of gaps and how each will be closed.

08

Working hypothesis

The formula “X for Y because Z”.

Sources

Primary data first

NSPD / EGRN

Property card, cadastral number, area, value.

Register extract

Rights, restrictions, current information.

Photos and plan

What is visible at the property and how it is laid out.

Validation

Every figure has a link to its source.

Rule: take physical parameters only from documents, not from memory or a presentation.
C52 example

A property can span several addresses

The cluster at Suvorova, 52 and 52a: two buildings, a courtyard, and three sea containers. The main building is the basis of the data sheet.

What to do when a property has several addresses

  1. Choose the main building — it is the basis of the data sheet (for C52, 52a).
  2. Add the other buildings, the courtyard, and temporary structures to the “Property composition” table.
  3. Check every record in the NSPD; if there is no record, mark it “not found in the register” and add it to “what we do not know”.
  4. Analyze the surroundings from the boundaries of the whole property. Sum the floor areas with a formula in the table.

C52 example; the same algorithm applies to your own property.

If the property does not qualify

Option 1

Option 2

Find another property of your own and check it against the same six criteria before Topic 1.1.

Not allowed: start with one property and replace it in the middle of the course.
Requirements

Requirements for your own property

  • An existing building or a site with buildings: redevelopment, not new construction.
  • A property in Russia: a cadastral number or address that locates a card in the NSPD.
  • Minimum documents: a register extract and a photo or plan; physical parameters only from documents.
  • You have the right to use the property data; anonymize confidential information.
  • The surroundings and audience can be analyzed from open data: 2GIS, OSM, Rosstat.
  • The property is chosen before Topic 1.1 and does not change until Demo Day (pilot).
If the property fails the check — work with the base C52.

Questions

What people usually ask

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my own property?
Yes, if it meets the requirements: an existing building or a site with buildings, a property in Russia, a cadastral number or address for the NSPD, and the minimum documents. If it does not, work with C52.
Can I change the property later?
No. The property is chosen before Topic 1.1 and does not change until Demo Day.
What if some documents are missing?
Record the gap in row 7 of the data sheet and state how you will close it. Do not invent physical parameters without a document.
